Generation is the core workflow in Editor AI. Open the panel, describe the level you want, and the AI builds it directly inside the Geometry Dash editor — placing objects on its own preview layer that you can accept, refine, or discard at any time.

## Starting a generation

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Editor AI panel">
    Press **E** anywhere in the game (desktop) or tap the floating **AI** bubble (mobile) to open the panel.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Go to the Generate tab">
    Inside the panel, select the **Generate** tab.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a target">
    Pick where the AI should build:
    - **Current editor** — works on the level you already have open
    - **New level** — creates a brand-new level and opens it automatically
    - **One of your created levels** — pick any level from your local list
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write a prompt">
    Describe the level you want in the prompt field. See [Prompt tips](#prompt-tips) below for guidance.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set Difficulty, Style, and Length">
    Adjust the three generation parameters (each accepts presets or free-form text — see details below).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Press Generate">
    Hit **Generate**. The session starts immediately and the AI begins working. You can watch progress in the **Sessions** tab, or close the panel and come back later.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Prompt tips

The AI reads your existing level before generating, so your prompt can reference what's already there.

- **Be descriptive about both gameplay and visuals.** "A hard cube level with tight spike timing and a dark industrial theme" gives the AI much more to work with than "a hard level."
- **Include mood, references, and special requests.** Try "Inspired by Cataclysm, with sawblades and flashing colors" or "Lo-fi aesthetic with pastel blocks and relaxed timing."
- **For follow-ups, say so explicitly.** Phrases like "extend this level" or "restyle this with a space theme" tell the AI to build on what exists rather than start fresh.

<Tip>
  Short prompts work too. Even "a spooky Halloween level" produces valid results. More detail gives better alignment with what you had in mind.
</Tip>

## Generation parameters

### Difficulty

Controls the target difficulty the AI aims for. Preset values are `easy`, `medium`, `hard`, and `extreme`, but any free-form text is accepted — try `demon`, `tutorial`, or `grandma-friendly`. Default: `medium`.

### Style

Sets the visual and gameplay style. Presets are `modern`, `retro`, `flow`, and `memory`. Free-form text is accepted here too — describe any aesthetic you have in mind. Default: `modern`.

### Length

Sets the approximate level length. Presets are `short`, `medium`, `long`, `xl`, and `xxl`. Free-form text is accepted if you want something more specific. Default: `medium`.

## Target Object Count

**Setting:** `target-object-count` — **Default:** 0 (off) — **Range:** 0–20,000

When set to a positive number, the AI is instructed to keep generating rounds until the total object count reaches at least that value. Leave it at 0 to let the AI decide when the level is complete. The hard cap is 20,000 objects.

## Quality Preset

**Setting:** `quality-profile` — **Default:** `premium`

Controls the tradeoff between speed and output quality.

| Preset | Behavior |
|---|---|
| `fast` | One quick draft pass — good for a rough sketch or testing prompts. |
| `balanced` | A moderate number of refinement passes — solid results without the full wait. |
| `premium` | *(Default)* Keeps refining until the AI itself reports the level is done. No fixed pass cap; cancel at any time. |
